**Temporary title (for journal search only, 12 words):** Median-Based Versus Standard-Deviation-Based R–R Irregularity for Atrial Fibrillation Screening in Short ECG

**Temporary abstract:** Irregular R–R intervals are the main electrocardiographic sign of atrial fibrillation (AF), but in short, noisy single-lead recordings extra or missed R peaks can inflate a standard-deviation-based irregularity measure. We compared the coefficient of variation (CV) with a median-based measure (MDR, median absolute deviation of R–R intervals divided by the median R–R interval) for research-oriented preliminary AF screening. We used 200 AF and 200 Normal records from the PhysioNet/CinC Challenge 2017 training set (first 100 per class for development, next 100 for testing), one fixed R-peak detector, and one threshold per measure chosen on development records only to maximise AF F1. On the 200 test records, F1 was 0.747 for CV and 0.826 for MDR, with specificity 0.34 and 0.72 and sensitivity 0.99 and 0.90. Swapping the roles of the two halves gave 0.752 and 0.833. The advantage appeared in all 200 random re-splits, but did not grow with signal noise, and records have no subject identifiers. The hypothesis that MDR is more robust to R-peak errors therefore remains untested. We plan independent validation on an external open database with thresholds frozen from the Challenge development set. This work does not diagnose atrial fibrillation.
